What the money is for

The whole budget behind the p(Doom)1 funding round, including the parts I cannot price yet.

The ask

p(Doom)1 is asking for a minimum of $14,500 on Manifund, with a goal of $48,000, and the round closes on 9 September 2026. It is all-or-nothing: below the minimum every pledge is returned and nobody is out of pocket. Asking for $14,500 without showing the working is a wish rather than a proposal, so this page is the working.

The live figures are on the Manifund page and are deliberately not typed here. They move daily, this page does not, and a stale number is worse than no number at all.

Neither figure buys a product. The game is free and source-available and stays that way — a pledge funds the work, not a licence, and not early access. p(Doom)1 is also not finished, which is rather the point of funding it.

The two numbers, and what each one changes

The difference between them is not scale. It is who does the work.

At $14,500, the project does not stop

  • $900 Hosting and infrastructure, 12 months
  • $1,100 Asset generation and tooling
  • $3,000 Open dataset maintenance, 12 months
  • $9,000 Partial recovery of Pip’s time

Nobody else is paid anything. The dataset stays maintained rather than drifting, and the art keeps improving the way it improved on 14 August: generate a great deal of it, throw most of it away, and write down why. That is a real answer to the art problem, but it is not the answer people mean when they ask about it.

At $48,000, somebody other than me draws it

  • $20,000 Part-time gameplay developer, ~6 months
  • $6,000 Directed art pass, human illustrator
  • $2,000 Community support

Every dollar of the human-artist line sits above the minimum, and at $14,500 none of it is committed. I could have spread that line across both columns to make the floor look better. The only public criticism this project has had is that the art looks too AI-generated, and answering that with a budget that quietly defers the answer would be worse than not answering at all.

Two gaps I would rather name than close

The minimum column sums to $14,000 against a published minimum of $14,500. That $500 is rounding from the evening the figure was set, not a hidden line item, and I would rather flag it than retrofit a line item to cover it.

Whether Manifund takes a platform fee is unknown and unasked. If there is one, the gap above is larger than $500, and it is better that I find that out than that you do.

A reader who adds the column up and finds it short with no acknowledgement concludes the table was reverse-engineered from the ask. One who finds the gap already flagged concludes the opposite, and is right.

The size of the art problem, measured rather than gestured at

On 14 August 2026 I judged 468 generated assets in about 23 minutes and discarded 273. That is one decision roughly every three seconds. That is the measurement the art line should be priced against, and it needs one correction first — a correction that cuts the number down rather than up.

273 discards is not 273 things that need drawing. The session was a selection sweep: several versions of the same slot competed and the losers were discarded, which is what the process is for. Decomposed properly, those judgements resolve to 198 distinct art slots — 194 with a surviving pick and 4 with nothing worth keeping at all. Anyone pricing a human pass should price 198 slots, not 273 discards, and I would rather publish the correction that lowers my own number than quote the one that flatters it.

The slots break down as:

  • 84 interface and action icons
  • 53 hero banners, key art and screen backgrounds
  • 12 researcher portraits
  • 49 further slots across smaller sets

The hero surfaces are the ones a stranger sees first, and they are where the sweep was harshest: 73 hero candidates judged, 19 kept.

Now the division, which is the part I would want to see in somebody else’s budget. $6,000 across 198 slots is about $30 a slot. I do not believe that is a real rate for commissioned illustration and I am not going to pretend otherwise. The shape I think the money actually buys is narrower and more useful. A style bible plus a directed pass over the ~53 hero and background slots, near $113 each, with the icon set regenerated against that direction rather than drawn by hand.

A hypothesis about what an illustrator would say. Not a plan anyone has agreed to. Precisely what a quote would settle.

Nobody has been hired

No artist, illustrator, developer or contractor has been engaged for this project. No rate has been agreed with anyone, and no approach has been made. Every figure on this page describes what the money would pay for if the round closes above the minimum; the conditional tense throughout is not hedging, it is accuracy.

If it does not fund, the pledges return, nobody loses anything, and I keep going at the rate I have been going at. The game does not stop; it stays a thing done in the gaps, the dataset stays a thing maintained in the gaps, and somebody other than me does not get to draw it. That is a real outcome and not a catastrophic one, and I would rather say so than manufacture an emergency.

The round closes 9 September 2026, all-or-nothing at $14,500. A pledge costs nothing unless it works.
